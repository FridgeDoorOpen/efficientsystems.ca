Principle 1 · v1.0 · figure to come

Architectures must be traversable by AI agents, and navigable by humans.

If an agent can't walk the structure of a system, it can't reason about it. If a person can't find their way through it, they won't trust what the agent says.

Most industrial architectures were never designed to be read. They were assembled one project at a time, and the knowledge of how the pieces connect lives in diagrams nobody updates and in people who have been there long enough. An AI agent pointed at that architecture finds tags without context and systems without relationships.

Traversable means every asset, system, data flow, and decision sits at a known coordinate, with explicit links to its neighbours. Navigable means the same structure is legible to a person. The two needs share one substrate, read through two interfaces: a dense instrument panel for experts, and a conversational layer for everyone else.

Where it comes from RAMI 4.0 and ISA-95 as the coordinate frame; ISA-101 high-performance HMI; the dense, honest interfaces of neil.computer.
