Every institutional process has two parts that share one name: the artifact (the form, the template, the sign-off) and the decision it was meant to support. Over time the artifact hardens into ritual, and the real decision moves into the head of whoever holds the paperwork. Automate the artifact and you remove the judgment that made it work.

Process archaeology asks four questions of each process. What decision does it serve? Who actually makes that decision? What context do they use that isn't on the form? What would break if they left? Some fences stand for good reasons, such as safety or regulation. Knowing which is the point.
