---
title: Incentives drive outcomes.
description: Systems mirror the org chart that builds them, and the org chart mirrors what each executive is measured on.
number: 3
version: "1.0"
lineage: Conway's law (Melvin Conway, 1968), applied to the reporting lines of asset-intensive companies.
history:
  - "1.0 · 2026-10-07 · First written."
---

Operations is measured on uptime, throughput, and safety. Technology is often measured on cost, standardization, and security. Finance is measured on the close and capital discipline. Each optimizes the systems it owns for its own measures, which is rational.

The operations layer suffers most. It usually has a functional owner but no systems owner: the people who need it report one way, and the systems they depend on are funded another. The point isn't that any one reporting structure is right. It's that a system owned by a line whose measures exclude its operational purpose will drift away from that purpose.
